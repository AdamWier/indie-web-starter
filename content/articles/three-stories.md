[Gilsbrecht Leythers - Winter Landscape with Travelers and Ice skaters](https://www.navigart.fr/museedartsdenantes/artwork/gysbrecht-leytens-paysage-d-hiver-avec-gitans-et-patineurs-110000000006033)

Snow had finally fallen and the town, as usual, took the first occasion to go ice skating on the lake. In their Breton town, the water froze almost instantly, though it was the custom of nervous mothers to fret as fathers coaxed their children onto the ice. Some for the very first time. For Louis, it was his first winter on the ice. It would also be his last. The ice would crack below his feet and he would look down, head cocked to the side with curiosity. Looking up and in the direction of his mother's scream before plunging into cold Hades' grip. !!She!! had finally been proven right.

[Niccolo Frangipane - The swaddled cat](https://www.navigart.fr/museedartsdenantes/artwork/niccolo-frangipane-attribue-a-le-chat-emmaillote-110000000004541)

"The cat! The cat! Bring the cat!" cried Gregory. The drunkards cheered unruly and discordantly. Gregory's gaze flew chaotically up, down and all around till Sam approached, Minette swaddled in a dirty kitchen rag, held lovingly if not awkwardly, in his arms. Minette, unsure what to make of the situation, struggled halfheartedly to get out of his arms, unsure if she thought the attention was worth the unpredictability of the unfolding events. "Humans are hardly worth the trouble," she thought with one last push from her fore-paws.

"Give her to me!" Nanette said between delirious giggles. "I'll feed the baby." The drunks cried out in jubilation once more.

"Oh no," thought Minette. Memories of last year rushed back with blazing speed.

"Feed the baby! Feed the baby!" cried the intoxicated drunkards.

"Oh dear god no," Minette thought again. Memories crystal clear now. 

"Open up for the porridge!"

Minette looked down with disdain at the spoon approaching her mouth. She could see from the corner of her eye the dumb dog laughing along with the humans, though he understood nothing of what was happening around him. Typical.

The spoon edged closer. It was bumping up against Minette's mouth, the wood was an unpleasant feeling. The "porridge" insulted her nostrils.

"I do not deserve this," she though. "But they deserve everything that's about to happen to them."

Minette closed her eyes and summoned from the depths of her soul the most horrendous song she could muster. One she would remember and later christen, "If you don't let me go right now, I will murder you and your progeny in their sleep so help me god." It was one for the books.

The humans covered their ears as Minette let out the loudest and most grating meow she had in her. Tendons taut and activated, little daggers moving through space, glinting in the candlelight.

Nanette dropped her. Something about her cheek and blood. Minette didn't care. She sauntered away, victorious. "Next year," she mused, "they better serve me salmon."

[AIZELIN (Eugène-Antoine) - The Hourglass Child, otherwise known as The Child Laughing at Time Passing](https://www.navigart.fr/museedartsdenantes/artwork/aizelin-eugene-antoine-aizelin-dit-l-enfant-au-sablier-dit-aussi-l-enfant-se-rit-du-temps-qui-passe-110000000004751?filters=authors%3AAIZELIN%20%28AIZELIN%20Eugene-Antoine,%20dit%29%E2%86%B9AIZELIN%20%28AIZELIN%20Eug%C3%A8ne-Antoine,%20dit%29)

She stood in a marble temple, before a podium, at the threshold. It had been a long journey to get here. And now that she found herself at the end of this road, she would soon advance forward one final time. She had prepared herself, step by step, for this last moment. In truth, it would be more of a leap than a step. A leap of faith. As she would not be here afterward to see the consequences of her actions, not really, she would never truly know if this was the right thing to do, if what was to come after would be any better. Throughout the year of travel to this very pedestal, she had repeated to herself a wordless mantra of self-sacrifice, of ultimate choice, of fate, even of resignation, but as she looked at the pedestal, placed in line with others, their figures broken and eroded by time and human action, she realized she had forgotten an important word of her unvoiced litany: hope. And another: generosity. And another: kindness. "It must have these things too," she whispered. She took a pomegranate from her bag. It was heavy in her hands. She massaged it as she approached the pedestal. One half for her. Splitting the pomegranate, she sucked the sweet juice from one half before letting it fall to the ground. And one half for... In a graceful and fluid motion, she took her place and held the other half out and up in her outstretched hand, offering it to everyone. A sweet and pure smile curved her lips before the stone set in and she turned marble white. This action was the first of a new world.
