Something I have done for over fifteen years now is to go to the museum and stream-of-consciousness write stories. I go with a notebook and pen, walk around, and when a work strikes me, I stand in front of it, and scribble out a a story as it comes to me. The idea to do this was inspired by an exercise I had in an improv class, back when I was studying theatre and film in an acting conservatory. We had to look at a piece of art and describe what we saw, allowing ourselves to embellish it into a story. I'm not a very visual guy. So when I went to museums, especially art museums, I never felt like I could properly appreciate the things I would see. I would look at them, think to myself, "this is nice" or "I like this color" and then move on. I didn't really feel I was connecting with the painting. I am, however, a very linguistic person. I read a lot up until my 20s, where I got distracted by other things, and now in my 40s, I'm getting back at it. I wrote a lot too. I studied translation and for a while, was a translator of poetry and literature for a small publisher and freelance. Even as young as six, I was writing, making books, and I have tried several times to write a novel. (And failed every time to date... One of these days!) I apparently also was an early speaker and reader, but how true that is and how much that's my mother exaggerating, as all mothers do, is up for debate, I think.

So when I attempted to reproduce this exercise at a museum one day, this time writing instead of speaking, it was like a whole new door opened for me. For the first time, I understood what it means when people say things like "dialoguing" or "having a conversation" with a piece of art. Not only did these little automatic writing exercises give me a focused way to look at and examine the art, not just noticing the details, but searching them out, needing them to feed the fire of the story. The art was telling me something and I needed to write it down. It wasn't just a static piece hanging on the wall. It was communicating something and something new, something that it could communicate, maybe, only to me. Even if the story came *out* of me, it was not created solely by me. It would not have existed without the piece divulging it to me as I copied it down, sometimes straining to hear as it whispered in my ear, messing up a few details, and sometimes deafening, so frantic and fast that it was impossible to get it all down at once.

I've never thought to share these stories until now. To me, they were always a private thing. Not out of potential embarrassment but more because it seemed unlikely to interest others. When first started jotting down these strange communiqués, I was writing in ways I deemed much more presentable. I managed to get a few plays out of me and onto the stage in my younger years. And I have a few other bits and pieces of myself floating around out there, essays, short stories, translations of poetry. In comparison, these little moments seemed more like an exercise or a means to an end. But now, over a decade later, the desire to share them has arisen in me. I'm not sure if this is because I have changed or society has. We share more now. In essence, this isn't bad when it's sincere. We should learn about each other. We should listen to each other. But unfortunately, most channels of transmission are not built to to encourage such open, honest, maybe even naked, confessions. They are built for other things. (I will let you think about what they are for on your own.) So it's curious that having left them for more healthy alternatives like the fediverse and indieweb, I now feel the urge to show some to you. But then again, I was always a bit of an exhibitionist.

Part of me is nervous to do this. Not because I don't think they're not good. (To be clear, they aren't good and never were meant to be.) But more because I am nervous of how this might transform my relationship to them, how it might affect future transmissions. Will I write the same knowing that someone might see them? Will the works, the future ones I have not seen, feel betrayed if they catch wind that I am relaying our secret conversation? Even more, have I already started writing differently from the moment the idea came to me, around a year ago? Even in these quiet moments in a museum, hearing a story being invented in real time by some other thing, I am still just as much a prisoner of our panopticon? I don't really know. But I figured this is one way to find out. Here below you will find posts that contain some of these stories, lightly edited. When possible, I have tried to include a photo of the artwork, its name and its author. You will note that sometimes the story has nothing to do with what the author might have meant, as I often look at the title after having written. This, as they say, is a feature and not a bug. I hope you enjoy them.
